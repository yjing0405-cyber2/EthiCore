Scenario 29: The Morality of Advertising
A marketing director wants to advertise a basic automated chatbot as a "Sentient AI," misleading customers about its capabilities.
Decision A (Ethical): Refuse the misleading campaign and market it as a "Smart Automated Assistant."
Immediate: Initial sales hype is lower than expected.
Explanation: Truthful advertising lacks the viral sensationalism of science-fiction promises.
Ripple: Users who buy it are satisfied because expectations match reality.
Explanation: Honest marketing ensures that the product fulfills the specific promises made to the consumer.
Long-Term: The product maintains a solid 5-star rating and steady long-term sales.
Explanation: Setting realistic expectations prevents buyer's remorse and builds sustainable brand equity.
Decision B (Unethical): Launch the "Sentient AI" campaign.
Immediate: Pre-orders and day-one sales break records.
Explanation: Blatant misrepresentation easily dupes the public into spending money on non-existent technology.
Ripple: Users quickly realize it's a basic script and feel scammed.
Explanation: The illusion shatters immediately upon use, resulting in intense anger and feelings of betrayal.
Long-Term: Class-action lawsuits for false advertising bankrupt the company.
Explanation: Severe consumer deception is heavily penalized by trade commissions and ruins the company entirely.
Decision C (Mixed): Use the term "AI-Powered" but bury the exact technical limitations in fine print.
Immediate: Sales are strong, and legal technically has cover.
Explanation: The company uses buzzwords to mislead while relying on the fine print to avoid outright fraud charges.
Ripple: Tech reviewers expose the marketing spin.
Explanation: Experts quickly dismantle misleading marketing terminology, warning consumers of the trick.
Long-Term: The company survives legally but loses all credibility among tech-savvy consumers.
Explanation: While avoiding lawsuits, the brand becomes synonymous with cheap gimmicks and untrustworthy marketing.
