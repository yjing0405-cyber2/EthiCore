Scenario 6: Refusing an ACM Code Violation
A software engineer is asked to write code that secretly throttles competitors' websites, violating ACM codes.
Decision A (Ethical): Refuse to write the code and cite ACM principles.
Immediate: The engineer is threatened with termination.
Explanation: Adhering to professional codes like the ACM often puts individuals at odds with unethical corporate directives.
Ripple: The engineer resigns and reports the company to a tech watchdog.
Explanation: Ethical professionals have a duty to not only refuse harmful work but to expose malicious corporate practices.
Long-Term: The company faces public backlash and sanctions, while the engineer finds a better job.
Explanation: Upholding ethical codes preserves the engineer's reputation, while the company faces the consequences of market manipulation.
Decision B (Unethical): Write the code for a bonus.
Immediate: The engineer receives the bonus.
Explanation: Financial incentives are frequently used to corrupt IT professionals into executing malicious actions.
Ripple: Competitors experience slowdowns and investigate the cause.
Explanation: Malicious network activities leave traces that competent technical adversaries will eventually uncover.
Long-Term: The engineer loses their professional credentials and faces potential legal action.
Explanation: Violating fundamental ethical codes permanently destroys a developer's professional standing and legal safety.
Decision C (Mixed): Write the code but add a backdoor to disable it later.
Immediate: The engineer keeps their job.
Explanation: This is an attempt to compromise, trying to satisfy the boss while planning an emergency exit strategy.
Ripple: The throttling causes chaos, and the engineer panics and triggers the backdoor.
Explanation: The developer loses control of the ethical compromise, leading to unpredictable system behavior.
Long-Term: The company discovers the backdoor, firing the engineer for insubordination.
Explanation: Trying to play both sides results in losing the trust of the employer without actually upholding ethical standards.
