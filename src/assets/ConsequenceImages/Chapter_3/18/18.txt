Scenario 18: Code Ownership Dispute
A developer creates a lucrative side-project app, but used a company laptop during lunch breaks to code part of it.
Decision A (Ethical): Disclose this to the employer and negotiate a royalty or ownership split.
Immediate: The developer loses full ownership of the app.
Explanation: Acknowledging the use of company resources requires surrendering a portion of intellectual equity.
Ripple: The company provides marketing resources to push the app.
Explanation: A negotiated partnership turns a legal vulnerability into a mutually beneficial corporate sponsorship.
Long-Term: The app succeeds massively, and both parties profit legally and fairly.
Explanation: Transparency ensures the developer reaps financial rewards without the looming threat of an ownership lawsuit.
Decision B (Unethical): Hide the fact and claim 100% ownership.
Immediate: The developer gets all early profits.
Explanation: Secrecy allows the developer to temporarily hoard all financial gains from the app.
Ripple: The company's IT audit discovers the source code on the laptop.
Explanation: Corporate IT policies routinely scan devices, making it impossible to hide side-projects built on company hardware.
Long-Term: The company sues, winning 100% of the IP rights, and the developer gets nothing.
Explanation: Legally, work done on company equipment usually belongs entirely to the employer, resulting in total loss for the developer.
Decision C (Mixed): Delete the code from the laptop and rewrite those specific modules at home.
Immediate: The developer loses weeks of progress.
Explanation: The developer attempts to "clean" their tracks by destroying the tainted code and doing double the work.
Ripple: The final product is slightly different and delayed.
Explanation: The rewrite causes setbacks but technically removes the company's hardware from the equation.
Long-Term: The developer keeps ownership but constantly fears a code-similarity audit.
Explanation: Even after a rewrite, the developer lives with anxiety that the logic was conceived on company time.
