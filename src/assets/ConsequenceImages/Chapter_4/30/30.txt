Scenario 30: The Problem of Fair Pricing
An IT repair shop receives a laptop with a disconnected hard drive cable. The owner is an elderly person who knows nothing about computers.
Decision A (Ethical): Reconnect the cable and charge a nominal, transparent diagnostic fee.
Immediate: The shop makes a small $20 profit.
Explanation: Ethical pricing charges only for the actual labor performed, ignoring the customer's ignorance.
Ripple: The elderly customer is extremely grateful and tells their family.
Explanation: Honesty builds immense goodwill, turning a minor repair into a powerful word-of-mouth marketing event.
Long-Term: The shop gains dozens of loyal, long-term customers through trust.
Explanation: Treating vulnerable customers fairly is the foundation of a highly respected, community-staple business.
Decision B (Unethical): Lie, say the motherboard is fried, and charge $500 for a "replacement."
Immediate: The shop makes a massive, easy profit.
Explanation: The technician ruthlessly preys on the customer's technical illiteracy to commit outright theft.
Ripple: The elderly customer struggles financially to pay the bill.
Explanation: Overcharging causes genuine financial harm to individuals living on fixed incomes.
Long-Term: The customer's tech-savvy grandson discovers the fraud and destroys the shop's reputation online.
Explanation: Predatory behavior is eventually exposed by knowledgeable third parties, destroying the shop's business.
Decision C (Mixed): Reconnect the cable but charge for a full "system optimization package" they didn't need.
Immediate: The shop makes $100 instead of $20.
Explanation: The shop avoids outright lying about the broken part but forcefully up-sells unnecessary services.
Ripple: The computer works, but the customer paid more than necessary.
Explanation: The customer gets a working laptop but is subtly exploited due to their lack of knowledge.
Long-Term: The shop becomes known for overcharging, driving away budget-conscious customers.
Explanation: A pattern of pushing unnecessary services creates a reputation for "nickel-and-diming" customers.
