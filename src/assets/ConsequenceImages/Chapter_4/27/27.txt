Scenario 27: The Problem of Just Wage
An IT agency wins a multimillion-dollar contract but plans to pay its data-entry contractors below minimum wage.
Decision A (Ethical): Insist on paying the contractors a legal, living wage from the massive profits.
Immediate: The agency's profit margin slightly decreases.
Explanation: Ethical compensation requires sacrificing maximum corporate greed to ensure basic human dignity for workers.
Ripple: The contractors are motivated and produce highly accurate data.
Explanation: Fair pay directly correlates with higher work quality, reliability, and employee morale.
Long-Term: The agency builds a reliable, loyal workforce and wins repeat contracts.
Explanation: A well-treated workforce provides superior service, leading to long-term business success.
Decision B (Unethical): Proceed with the exploitative pay rate to maximize profit.
Immediate: The agency's executives get huge bonuses.
Explanation: Exploitation enriches those at the top at the direct expense of the laborers doing the work.
Ripple: Contractors realize they are being exploited and work carelessly.
Explanation: Underpaid workers have no incentive to care about data integrity, leading to massive quality drops.
Long-Term: The client rejects the low-quality data, and the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) fines the agency.
Explanation: The strategy backfires when poor outputs ruin the contract and the government punishes the wage theft.
Decision C (Mixed): Pay minimum wage, but classify them as "interns" to avoid paying benefits.
Immediate: Labor costs are kept artificially low.
Explanation: This exploits legal loopholes regarding worker classification to avoid paying health and social benefits.
Ripple: The contractors eventually leave for better jobs.
Explanation: Workers will use the "internship" as a stepping stone and abandon the exploitative company at the first opportunity.
Long-Term: The agency suffers from constant turnover and retraining costs.
Explanation: The money saved on benefits is eventually lost to the constant friction of hiring and training new staff.
