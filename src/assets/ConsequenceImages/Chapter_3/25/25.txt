Scenario 25: Cybersquatting a Trademark
A webmaster realizes a famous international shoe brand hasn't registered its .ph domain and buys it to hold for ransom.
Decision A (Ethical): Hand over the domain at cost or let it expire.
Immediate: The webmaster loses the small registration fee.
Explanation: Relinquishing the domain means taking a negligible financial loss to respect another entity's trademark.
Ripple: The brand secures its domain without issue.
Explanation: The brand establishes its legitimate digital presence in the country smoothly.
Long-Term: The webmaster avoids WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization) arbitration.
Explanation: Stepping away prevents the webmaster from being drawn into complex international legal disputes.
Decision B (Unethical): Demand $50,000 from the brand for the domain.
Immediate: The brand's legal team is notified.
Explanation: Extortion via cybersquatting instantly triggers a hostile legal response from corporate entities.
Ripple: The brand files a UDRP (Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy) complaint.
Explanation: International domain registrars have specific, aggressive protocols to seize ransomed trademarked domains.
Long-Term: The webmaster loses the domain forcefully and pays legal penalties for bad-faith registration.
Explanation: Cybersquatting is explicitly illegal, resulting in the forceful forfeiture of the asset and punitive fines.
Decision C (Mixed): Build a fake fan site on the domain to make it look "legitimate" before selling it.
Immediate: The webmaster spends hours building a dummy site.
Explanation: The webmaster tries to create a facade of legitimate use to bypass cybersquatting laws.
Ripple: The brand still files a dispute, arguing trademark infringement.
Explanation: Corporate lawyers easily see through fake sites designed solely to protect squatted domains.
Long-Term: The webmaster wastes time and money, eventually losing the domain in arbitration anyway.
Explanation: Feigning legitimate use rarely stands up to scrutiny in international trademark arbitration.
