Scenario 5: Taking Credit for Junior's Code
A senior developer presents an innovative algorithm written by an intern as their own to management.
Decision A (Ethical): Immediately credit the intern during the presentation.
Immediate: The intern receives praise from management.
Explanation: Honest attribution immediately validates the hard work of junior staff.
Ripple: The intern is highly motivated and hired full-time.
Explanation: Recognizing talent fosters loyalty and encourages further innovation from new employees.
Long-Term: The senior developer is respected as a great mentor and leader.
Explanation: True professional leadership is built on lifting up team members, not stealing their accomplishments.
Decision B (Unethical): Accept the praise and take full credit.
Immediate: The senior developer gets a raise.
Explanation: Plagiarism can yield immediate financial or reputational rewards if undetected.
Ripple: The intern discovers the theft and quits in frustration.
Explanation: Intellectual theft destroys trust and drives valuable talent away from the organization.
Long-Term: The senior developer cannot maintain the complex code, leading to system failures and their eventual termination.
Explanation: Claiming credit for code you don't understand inevitably leads to technical failure when maintenance is required.
Decision C (Mixed): Share the credit vaguely ("My team and I built this").
Immediate: The senior developer still gets the majority of the praise.
Explanation: Obfuscating exact contributions allows the senior dev to technically avoid lying while still absorbing undue credit.
Ripple: The intern feels undervalued but stays quiet.
Explanation: Partial credit breeds resentment and stifles the junior employee's motivation to innovate.
Long-Term: The intern leaves for a competitor, taking their innovative ideas with them.
Explanation: Failing to properly champion junior talent ultimately benefits competitor organizations.
