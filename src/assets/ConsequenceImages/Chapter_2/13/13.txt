Scenario 13: Ignoring a Subpoena
An IT director receives a valid court subpoena for user logs related to a criminal investigation but the CEO says to ignore it.
Decision A (Ethical): Comply with the subpoena and hand over the logs.
Immediate: The CEO is furious.
Explanation: Complying with law enforcement often puts IT leadership in direct conflict with self-protecting executives.
Ripple: The authorities use the logs to catch a dangerous cybercriminal.
Explanation: IT logs are critical pieces of digital forensics necessary for the justice system to function.
Long-Term: The IT director is protected by law, and the company is seen as cooperative.
Explanation: Upholding legal obligations protects the individual professional and keeps the company clear of obstruction charges.
Decision B (Unethical): Delete the logs and claim a system error.
Immediate: The CEO is pleased.
Explanation: The IT director commits spoliation of evidence to appease corrupt upper management.
Ripple: Investigators find digital forensics proving the intentional deletion.
Explanation: Deleting data leaves traces; forensic experts can easily differentiate a system error from deliberate wiping.
Long-Term: The IT director is jailed for obstruction of justice.
Explanation: Following illegal orders from a CEO does not shield an IT professional from severe criminal penalties.
Decision C (Mixed): Hand over heavily redacted logs.
Immediate: Authorities are frustrated by the lack of useful data.
Explanation: The IT director attempts malicious compliance, legally submitting a document stripped of value.
Ripple: A judge orders the unredacted logs to be surrendered under threat of contempt.
Explanation: The legal system does not tolerate gamesmanship regarding subpoenaed digital evidence.
Long-Term: The company wastes time and money on legal fees before inevitably complying.
Explanation: Trying to skirt legal obligations only delays the inevitable and angers the judiciary.
